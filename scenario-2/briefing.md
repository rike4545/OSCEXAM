# Scenario 2 — NYC / Municipal Labor Committee Health Benefits Agreement (FY 2019–2021)

## Executive summary

In a June 28, 2018 agreement, the City of New York and the Municipal Labor Committee (MLC) established a healthcare-savings commitment of **$1.1 billion over FY 2019–FY 2021**: $200 million in FY 2019, $300 million in FY 2020, and $600 million in FY 2021. The agreement also required **$600 million in recurring annual savings thereafter**, to be achieved and agreed upon by the parties within FY 2021. Savings were measured against the City's June 15, 2018 Financial Plan assumptions for healthcare cost growth (7% in FY 2019, 6.5% in FY 2020, and 6% in FY 2021). The agreement allowed limited transfers of nonrecurring savings among the three target years, but the ongoing $600 million obligation had to be recurring and could not be borrowed from another year. A key accounting mechanism was to credit certain health-plan savings to the City by reducing the City's equalization payments to the Joint Health Insurance Stabilization Fund (HISF), rather than requiring every credited dollar to appear as a direct reduction in claims or cash benefit expense. The agreement also established actuarial data-sharing and joint policy-review processes, specified payments to welfare funds, and created a framework for allocating recurring savings above the $600 million threshold.

## Major provisions

- **Savings targets:** $200 million (FY 2019), $300 million (FY 2020), and $600 million (FY 2021), totaling $1.1 billion over the three-year period; $600 million per year was to continue on a recurring basis thereafter.
- **Baseline and timing:** Savings were measured against the City's June 15, 2018 Financial Plan, which assumed healthcare cost increases of 7%, 6.5%, and 6% in FY 2019, FY 2020, and FY 2021, respectively. Certain nonrecurring savings could be shifted among the three years, but the recurring $600 million annual amount could not be borrowed from another year.
- **Equalization-payment offsets:** Savings attributable to the Citywide Benefits Program (CBP), documented by Empire Blue Cross and GHI, could be transferred to the City by offsetting the amounts against the City's equalization payments to the Stabilization Fund for FY 2019–FY 2021. To discontinue that offset, replacement savings had to be found to preserve the recurring obligation. This is a budget/accounting credit against a payment otherwise due to the Fund; it should not automatically be described as an equivalent reduction in medical claims or direct cash healthcare spending.
- **Prior-period savings credited forward:** Savings above the $3.4 billion target under the 2014 agreement for FY 2015–FY 2018 were eligible to count toward the FY 2019 goal (then estimated at approximately $131 million, subject to final actuarial review). Recurring savings above $1.3 billion for FY 2018 under the prior agreement were also eligible to count toward the 2019–2021 goals and ongoing obligation (then estimated at approximately $40 million, subject to final actuarial review).
- **Use of excess recurring savings:** After FY 2021, the parties were to calculate realized savings. If recurring savings exceeded $600 million, the first $68 million was to fund a $100 per-member-per-year increase in welfare-fund contributions for active employees and retirees, effective July 1, 2021 (prorated if the excess was between $0 and $68 million). Additional savings thereafter were to be split equally between the City and the MLC, with use determined by agreement.
- **Data and oversight:** Beginning January 1, 2019, quarterly claims-level data from EmblemHealth and Empire Blue Cross and HIP-HMO utilization data were to be provided to both sides' actuaries within 60 days after each quarter. The agreement required baseline data and protection of member privacy through a data-sharing agreement.
- **Joint policy review:** A Tripartite Health Insurance Policy Committee (City, MLC, and a jointly designated chair) was to examine plan design, financing, prescription-drug purchasing, Medicare Advantage for retirees, audits, coordination of benefits, Centers of Excellence, provider tiering, and other cost-control options. Recommendations were to be considered by the City and MLC.
- **Welfare-fund payments:** The Joint Stabilization Fund was to fund one-time $100 per-member payments to welfare funds for active employees and retirees, effective July 1, 2018 and July 1, 2019.
- **Dispute resolution:** Disputes under specified provisions were subject to meet-and-confer and, if unresolved, referral to the designated neutral for resolution under the agreement's dispute-resolution framework.

## Analyst's explanation of the accounting mechanism

The agreement's equalization-payment provision matters because it changes the City's net cash transfer to the HISF. In simplified terms, if an equalization payment would otherwise be due from the City to the Fund, an approved savings offset reduces that payment. The City therefore receives budgetary/financial-plan value through a smaller transfer obligation. That mechanism is analytically distinct from demonstrating that the underlying health plan paid fewer claims or that the City's gross healthcare expenditure fell by the same amount. A careful briefing should distinguish (1) reductions in projected cost growth or actual claims costs, (2) offsets to equalization payments, and (3) recurring savings that continue in later years. The actuaries' baseline, assumptions, data, and reconciliation are central to validating that the same savings are not counted twice and that a one-time offset is not mistaken for a recurring reduction.

## Briefing points for the Deputy

1. The agreement front-loads a defined savings schedule, reaching $600 million in FY 2021, while also requiring that $600 million annual savings continue after FY 2021.
2. The most important accounting nuance is that some savings could be credited through offsets to City equalization payments to the HISF. That reduces the City's transfer obligation but is not, by itself, proof of an equal reduction in gross claims spending.
3. The agreement relies on joint actuarial review, regular claims/utilization data, and a policy committee to identify and validate savings.
4. The treatment of prior-period savings and the rules for transferring nonrecurring savings make baseline controls and recurring-versus-one-time classification especially important.

## Scope note

This briefing summarizes the June 28, 2018 agreement text. It does not assess whether the parties ultimately achieved the targets or whether the Fund remained financially sustainable. Those are separate retrospective questions requiring subsequent financial and audit evidence.
