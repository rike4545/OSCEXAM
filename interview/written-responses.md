## 1. Please describe what life experiences have helped you prepare for this job.

My preparation for this role comes from a combination of formal education, direct public-sector experience, hands-on financial administration, and operational leadership. I hold an MBA and an MPP from Stony Brook University, as well as a bachelor's degree in political science. That combination has helped me understand both the mechanics of financial management and the public-policy choices behind government budgets. I also had the opportunity to serve as Town of Riverhead Budget Officer and Deputy Chief of Staff, which gave me direct exposure to municipal operations and the need to connect financial information with decisions made by elected officials and department leadership. Earlier in my career, as a financial and human-resources assistant, I worked with a $3 million land-acquisition budget, processed and reconciled financial transactions, prepared management reports, and conducted quarterly reviews that identified discrepancies and produced approximately $50,000 in annual savings. My temporary work with the New York State Comptroller further exposed me to audit standards, compliance, and the importance of clearly documenting findings. In healthcare administration at Stony Brook University Hospital, I performed revenue-versus-expenditure forecasting and supported quality-assurance work. More recently, my management roles have strengthened my ability to supervise teams, monitor costs, improve processes, and maintain controls in a fast-paced environment. I have also continued to apply my public-finance interests through an independent Riverhead budget-information project that organizes public budgets, audits, payroll, debt, and legislative records into a searchable analytical resource. Collectively, these experiences have prepared me to approach municipal financial analysis with technical care, an understanding of government operations, and an appreciation for the public consequences of fiscal decisions.

## 2. Please describe an example of an analysis you have done. Please include the data source(s), analytical approach, software used, and conclusion in your response.

One example is the development of an independent municipal budget-analysis and public-information platform focused on the Town of Riverhead. The purpose was to make complex public financial information easier to compare, reconcile, and explain. The source material included the Town's adopted and proposed budgets, annual financial reports and audits, budget account data, payroll and authorized-position information, debt and capital records, resolutions and voting records, and relevant New York State public data. I approached the work as a data-reconciliation and trend-analysis project: I collected records from different publication formats, extracted and normalized account and fiscal-year information, matched related records, checked totals and classifications, and organized the results so that users could compare budgeted amounts with actual results, review changes over time, and examine the fiscal context of policy decisions. I used Python for extraction, transformation, normalization, and data-quality checks; SQL for structured querying and investigation; and JavaScript/TypeScript with a Next.js interface to make the resulting information searchable and accessible. The key conclusion from the work was that a useful municipal analysis cannot stop at presenting a single budget number. It must explain the underlying account classifications, distinguish recurring operating resources from one-time resources, reconcile budget figures to reported results, and show how policy choices affect taxes, reserves, staffing, debt, and future obligations. The project reinforced the importance of traceable sources, consistent definitions, and presenting quantitative findings in a form that decision-makers and residents can understand.
